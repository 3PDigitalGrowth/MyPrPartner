Who gets which EDM

The follow-up to our discussion with Lyall, worked through to the send files. Schools hear about the Schools Program, and the crisis sequence gets pointed at the audience Search Console says is already looking for it.

The ruling this plan implements

Schools receive Schools Program content only. No school on any list receives a Crisis Masterclass pitch, in any email, at any point in a sequence.

Messaging alignment

Each audience hears about the program built for it

The direction we settled on with Lyall is a clean one: principals hear about the Schools Program, and the Crisis Masterclass goes to the audiences it was priced and packaged for. Everything below is that direction carried through to the sequences, the landing pages and the send files.

The Schools Program is the stronger story for that reader in any case. Twelve modules of school-specific material, allegations handling with Tim Sterne, board and governance risk with Tim Whincop, a whole-team licence rather than one seat. It answers the question a principal is actually holding, which is how the leadership team responds together, not how one person gets trained.

The alignment also does compliance work. Cold outreach here rests on inferred consent through conspicuous publication, and one of the three conditions is that the message is directly relevant to the recipient's role. A schools-specific program sent to a principal at a published school address passes that test cleanly. A general business training subscription sent to the same address is a weaker argument on the day someone asks the question.

What that changes in Sequence A Three things move together. The price anchor becomes $440 to $1,340 a month per school rather than $340 a seat, the buyer becomes the school rather than the principal personally, and the proof stack leads on fifteen years of CRC schools work rather than the Crisis Ready Institute licence.

Reading it

Google puts myprpartner.com in the top ten for the subscription phrasings and at position 50 to 75 for the workshop phrasings. Both facts are the same fact: the site is built around a recurring training subscription, and the phrases that describe a recurring training subscription are the ones nobody else in the Australian market is writing pages for. Every competitor in this market sells a facilitator day.

On company size, Search Console reports no firmographics at all, so the size read has to come from the language people type. Every query in the set that names a buyer size names a small one: smes twice, small business or small businesses four times, startups once, business owners twice. Nothing across 333 queries names enterprise, corporate group, ASX or multinational in a training context. The enterprise-flavoured queries that do appear ("financial services pr agency", "professional services pr", "corporate services pr agency") are agency-hire intent rather than training-purchase intent, and every one of them sits past position 50.

Price is the second, independent signal, and it brackets the same range. At $340 to $440 a month the purchase sits inside one manager's discretion. Above roughly 500 staff an organisation has an in-house communications function and buys agency retainers instead. Below about ten staff the recurring line does not survive a slow quarter. That gives a working target of 10 to 500 employees, which happens to be a cut the master list supports directly, because the vendor tags every row with an employee band.

Worth a decision Not-for-profit is the second largest organic cluster in the dataset, ahead of schools and associations combined. The Charity program page is currently hidden and redirects to /programs/business, and there is no NFP segment in any purchased list. That is demand with nowhere to land.

9 September addendum: the fifth tier

Lyall asked where the businesses outside the four categories go
Why
The 2 September cut chose four verticals with an obvious crisis story and left the rest of the 10 to 500 band unrouted. That was roughly half the decision-maker pool. Tier 5, general business, closes the gap with one extra opener; the shared body is already sector-neutral.
Tier 4
Narrowed to construction, mining and energy, where the site-incident opener belongs. Transport, security and fitness move to Tier 5.
Tier 5
Business services, IT and software, manufacturing, retail, consultants, architects, engineers, transport, security, fitness and every other in-band industry outside Tiers 1 to 4. Cleaned harder than the others: agency, media, comms and recruitment names (2,862), known enterprise names (2,128) and firms with ten or more contacts on the list (9,656, a branch-office headcount rather than a 10 to 500 business) were dropped.
Dual purpose
Tier 5 splits by job title into two files with their own openers. 5a is owners and leaders: CEO, MD, GM, director, founder. 5b is the comms professional inside the same businesses: marketing managers and directors, brand managers, communications managers, heads of marketing, social media managers. For 5b the title filter widens past the decision-maker pattern, because in a 50-person business the marketing manager is the comms function. About 300 of the 5b rows are coordinator, assistant or executive titles, kept for now as forwarders and easy to drop. Every file carries a persona column, so the same split can be applied to Tiers 1 to 4 without re-cutting; between them those four files already hold 1,710 comms-persona titles.

Execution

Order of work

  1. Sign off the copy

    Every sequence is written and sitting in the Copy Desk waiting on approval. Nothing sends until Lyall and Barbara have been through each cohort and pressed Approve. That is the gate on the whole programme, so it is the first thing rather than the last.

  2. Build Sequence A around the Schools Program

    Four emails, school-specific proof and price anchor throughout, no Crisis Masterclass mention. Independent and Catholic variants differ on one paragraph: the Catholic version acknowledges that the diocese may want the conversation.

  3. Run the sector enrichment and cut the schools file

    Domain classification across the 5,477 blank-sector rows, then a split into the 3,061 send file and the 7,088 nurture file. The 1,058 unresolved rows get a manual pass rather than a guess.

  4. Cut the business pilot at 4,500

    750 from each of the six opener files, largest organisations first, measured on reply rate rather than open rate. Six files at equal size is a test, not a blast, and it answers which vertical, and which persona, to spend the other 84,000 on.

  5. Record the consent basis per file

    For councils, the published address and source URL for all 523. For schools, role-published addresses preferred. For business, the tier and title that establish role relevance. This is the record that matters if ACMA ever asks, and it is cheap to keep while the files are being cut.

  6. Send, on the cold stack, not Kajabi

    Day 0, 3, 7, 10. Tuesday to Thursday, 7:30 to 9:00am recipient state time. UTM per segment per email. Suppression list live from the first send, and any inbox past 5% bounce pauses immediately. Kajabi stays out of it: a purchased-list send through the platform that runs the checkout risks the paid members' account.

Open

Five things I need from you

When can Lyall and Barbara get through the copy?
All six cohorts are drafted and waiting in the Copy Desk. Approval is the only thing between here and a first send, and the list work in steps 2 to 4 can run alongside it.
Government schools: nurture, or hold entirely?
My recommendation is one value-only email carrying the crisis communication plan resource, then silence. It builds a named list for a department-level approach without burning 7,088 principals on an ask they cannot approve.
Does the Charity program come back?
Not-for-profit is the second largest organic cluster on the site and the page currently sits behind a redirect. Either un-hide it and give the demand somewhere to land, or accept that the traffic goes to competitors while it stays hidden.
Business program pricing, still TBA?
The first send to the business tiers sells the Crisis Masterclass at $340 and collects waitlist signups. If pricing lands before the send, the sequence gets materially stronger, because the business program is the better fit for most of those 43,000 organisations.
Sign-off on the 10 to 500 employee band?
It is the single filter doing the most work, and the one most open to argument. Widening to 500 to 1,000 adds roughly 30,000 contacts, and my read is that those organisations buy agency retainers rather than training subscriptions.
